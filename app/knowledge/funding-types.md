# Choosing the right type of funding

## Grants
Non-dilutive money with conditions. Best for ideas with social, environmental or research value, and for early stages where risk is too high for investors. Expect reporting, fixed budgets and slower decisions. Competitive: match the funder's mission closely.

## Equity investment
Money in exchange for ownership. Suits businesses that can grow very large quickly. Investors expect a path to a big exit and will want board rights or information rights. Not suitable for steady lifestyle or local businesses.

## Prizes and competitions
Small amounts plus visibility, mentoring and introductions. Good for early-stage ideas and for building credibility that helps later applications. Usually judged on a short pitch.

## Accelerators
Fixed programs (often 3-6 months) with mentoring, a network and sometimes a small investment for equity. Selection weighs team commitment, coachability and progress speed.

## Loans and debt
For businesses with revenue and cash flow that can make repayments. Cheaper than equity if the business is stable. Lenders look at trading history, margins, existing debt and collateral or guarantees. Revenue-based finance repays as a share of monthly revenue.
