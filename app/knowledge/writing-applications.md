# Writing a strong funding application

## Lead with the point
Open with one sentence that says what you do, for whom, and the result: "We help 400 rice farmers cut irrigation water by 18%". Reviewers decide early whether to read closely.

## Specific beats impressive
Replace adjectives with numbers and names: how many customers, which town, what price, what date. "Fast-growing" means nothing; "grew from 120 to 410 paying shops in five months" means a lot.

## Tailor to each funder
Mirror the funder's own criteria and vocabulary. A climate grant wants outcomes in tonnes or hectares; an equity fund wants growth and market size; an accelerator wants coachability and commitment. Reusing one generic text is the most common mistake.

## Be honest about gaps
State risks and how you will address them. Panels trust applicants who show they understand their weaknesses more than ones who claim to have none. Never invent figures; write what you know and what you will measure.

## Make the ask concrete
State the exact amount, what it pays for, and the milestone it reaches. Tie it to time: "This funds 9 months to reach 1,500 farmers and break even at the farmer-group level."
