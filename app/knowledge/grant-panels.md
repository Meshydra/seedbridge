# How grant panels score applications

## Common scoring criteria
Most grant panels score a handful of criteria, often weighted: the significance of the problem and who benefits, the quality and feasibility of the proposed approach, the capability of the team, value for money of the budget, measurable outcomes, and what happens after the grant ends (sustainability). Reviewers read many applications quickly, so the strongest ones answer each criterion directly, in the panel's own words.

## Eligibility comes first
Applications are often removed before scoring for eligibility reasons: wrong location, wrong organisation type, wrong sector, asking for more than the maximum, missing documents or a missed deadline. Always check the eligibility list line by line before writing.

## Budgets and value for money
Panels want a budget that maps each cost to an activity and each activity to an outcome. Round numbers with no breakdown, large unexplained "miscellaneous" lines and salaries with no deliverables lose points. Co-funding or in-kind contributions show commitment.

## Impact and outcomes
Grant funders distinguish outputs (things you produce, like training sessions held) from outcomes (changes for people, like income increased). Good applications name a baseline, a target, a timeframe and how it will be measured.

## Common reasons for rejection
Vague problem statements, no evidence that the target group wants the solution, an approach that has already failed elsewhere with no explanation of what is different, a team without the needed skills, and budgets that do not add up.
