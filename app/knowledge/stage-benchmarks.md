# Funding stage benchmarks

## Just an idea
Funders at this stage back the founder and the problem, not the numbers. Strong applications show the founder has spoken to at least 20-30 potential customers, can describe one painful moment in their day, and has a cheap way to test demand (a landing page, a manual pilot, pre-orders). Typical sources: personal savings, family and friends, university and government innovation grants, idea-stage competitions and pre-accelerators. Asks are usually small and tied to a single learning milestone.

## Pre-seed
Pre-seed funders expect a working prototype or first version, a clear first customer segment and early signals of pull: a waitlist, pilot users, letters of intent or first paid customers. The team should cover building and selling. Typical sources: angel investors, accelerators, pre-seed funds and larger grants. The money should buy 12-18 months to reach product-market-fit signals, and use of funds should be mostly product and first sales.

## Seed
Seed investors look for evidence that customers want the product: steady revenue or usage growth (often 10-20% month on month), retention, and a repeatable way to acquire customers with known cost. They ask about market size (a credible path to a large outcome), unit economics and why this team wins. Typical sources: seed funds, angel syndicates, revenue-based finance and impact investors. Use of funds usually targets growth hires and go-to-market for 18-24 months.

## Series A and beyond
Series A investors fund scaling of something that already works. They expect meaningful recurring revenue, efficient growth, a management team beyond the founders, solid financial reporting and a plan to win a large market. Weak spots that block a round: concentration in one customer, high churn, unclear margins.

## Live business (trading)
An operating business seeking growth capital is judged on financial track record: revenue history, margins, cash flow, existing debt and the specific growth project the money funds. Loans, working-capital finance, equipment finance, grants for expansion or export, and impact investors are common fits. Lenders focus on repayment ability; equity investors on growth potential.
