# What equity investors look for

## Team
Early-stage investors repeatedly name the team as the main factor: relevant experience or unusual insight into the problem, complementary skills (building and selling), commitment (full time), and evidence of speed and learning.

## Market
Investors need a path to a large outcome. A bottom-up market size (number of customers you can reach times what each pays) is more credible than a top-down industry figure. They also ask why now: what changed in technology, regulation or behaviour that makes this possible today.

## Traction
Traction is the strongest evidence a founder can show: revenue, growth rate, retention, engagement, pilots that converted to paid, and signed letters of intent. Numbers should include a timeframe and a trend, not just a total.

## Business model and unit economics
Investors ask who pays, how much, how often, what it costs to win a customer and what each customer is worth over time. Gross margin and payback period matter more as the company grows.

## Defensibility
Why can't a larger company copy this? Answers include proprietary data, network effects, distribution partnerships, regulation, brand or deep expertise.

## Red flags
Unrealistic projections with no basis, very large asks at very early stages, founders who are part time, no clear customer, unclear ownership of the company or its intellectual property, and claims that cannot be verified.
